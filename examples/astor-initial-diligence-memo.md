# Astor — initial diligence example

> **Status:** Illustrative first-pass research note, not an investment recommendation. This is based on the company's public funding announcement only; it is not independent verification of customers, retention, product performance, valuation or round terms.

## Snapshot

- **Company:** Astor
- **Reported financing:** $5.0M seed
- **Announcement date:** April 23, 2026
- **Reported investors:** Monashees (lead), Y Combinator, Goodwater Capital, Gilgamesh Ventures, 468 Capital
- **Sector hypothesis:** fintech / wealth technology
- **Primary source:** https://www.astor.app/blog/astor-raises-5m-seed

## Initial hypothesis

The company describes an AI-powered investing advisory product. A plausible wedge is helping consumers move from fragmented investment information to a personalised portfolio workflow. That is a hypothesis derived from the category and company description—not evidence of user demand, advice quality or retention.

## What must be established

1. Who is the initial customer: self-directed investor, affluent household, advisor, or another buyer?
2. What workflow is materially better than existing broker, advisor and portfolio tools?
3. What proportion of users connect accounts, fund accounts and return after 30/90 days?
4. Is the product advice, education, portfolio analytics, or execution—and what licenses/disclosures apply?
5. What is the revenue model and who pays?
6. What is customer acquisition cost by channel and payback period?
7. How is advice quality assessed, and what happens when model output is wrong?
8. What is the regulatory perimeter in each market served?
9. What data rights, security controls and model-vendor dependencies exist?
10. What milestone does the seed round finance, and what runway does that imply?

## Early scorecard

All nine dimensions remain unassessed until an analyst records a 1–5 rating with written evidence. The financing announcement alone does not justify ratings for market size, founder edge, traction, defensibility or capital efficiency.

## Disconfirming evidence to seek

- Users do not fund or retain assets after onboarding.
- Advice does not outperform simpler low-cost alternatives on the target customer's actual objective.
- Acquisition depends on paid channels with unattractive payback.
- Regulatory obligations make the intended product or economics impractical.
- The defensible asset is not proprietary data, trust, distribution or a workflow advantage, but only a thin LLM interface.

## Preliminary disposition

**INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE.** Verify company claims, request customer and cohort metrics, inspect the product, understand compliance, and review founder references, cap table, valuation and financing terms before forming an investment view.
